## topic: How the exam works | how-exam-works
The exam asks one question at a time, getting harder, until you start guessing. That shows your real level. The point is not to pass it: it is to find your gaps while it is cheap to fix them.

You answer **from memory, in your own words**, with no notes. You need at least **80%** (8 out of 10) and you need the flashcards.

The exam covers only what Phase 0 taught:
- what Python, Node, Git and Docker are each for
- Git vs GitHub
- image vs container
- what `-p 5679:5678` means
- what a volume protects
- why a venv
- why `.env` is in `.gitignore`

Everything on that list is in the lessons of p0-1 to p0-5. If a topic feels shaky, open that lesson's topic, answer its *Check yourself* questions, and tick only what you really know.

## topic: Diagnosis and flashcards | diagnosis-flashcards
After the exam, the **diagnostician** looks at your mistakes (from the exam and from `mistakes.md`) and looks for the **one root misunderstanding** behind several of them. Fixing the root fixes many mistakes at once.

The **clerk** then turns the mistakes into **flashcards**: a question on one side, the answer on the other. You review them right before you would forget them. This is **spaced repetition**: the same day, the next day, after 3 days, a week, then two weeks. The free app **Anki** does the spacing for you (AnkiDroid on Android, AnkiMobile on iPhone, Anki Web). Claude saves the cards as a tab-separated text file (question, tab, answer), which Anki imports with File, Import. The quiz page on the dashboard works the same way in your browser.
