## topic: Open with their problem | open
Clients read **the first two lines** in the notification. Those lines decide whether they open the rest. **Open with their problem in your words**, not with "Hello, I'm an expert".

Weak: "Hello, I'm an experienced automation expert with 5 years..." (you have no 5 years, and it says nothing about them).
Strong: "You're losing track of waitlist signups because they sit in a database nobody checks. Here's how I'd fix it."

**Never paste the same text into every proposal.** Clients can tell, and it costs you Connects for nothing.

## topic: The five-part structure (about 120 words) | structure
1. **One sentence showing you understood their problem.**
2. **One sentence on how you would approach it.**
3. **One relevant project, with a link** (your case study).
4. **One clarifying question** that shows you've thought about it.
5. **A clear next step**, such as a 15-minute call.

Example:

> You're missing new waitlist signups because nobody checks the table. I'd poll it every 15 minutes, log each signup to a sheet and email you once per signup, tested on sample data before go-live. I built exactly this as a practice project: [link]. One question: where are the signups stored today? Happy to talk for 15 minutes this week.

## topic: Promises you can keep | promise
**Don't promise results you can't control.** "I'll make you rank first" or "this will double sales" is a promise you can't keep. A promise you can keep: "I'll set it up so each new signup is logged and emailed to you within 15 minutes, and I'll test it with sample data before we go live."

**Don't pay for Connects on jobs with red flags** (p5-3). Never accept a "test project" for free.

**Only you send proposals.** Claude checks them as the client; you fix each comment yourself, and you press send.

## topic: The three-proposal exercise | exercise
1. Pick your **three best jobs** from p5-3. Write each proposal in `proposals.md`.
2. Run the **Checker prompt** on each. Claude plays the client who posted that job and says what it noticed first, whether it would reply, what felt generic, and what you couldn't deliver.
3. Fix every comment yourself.
4. When Claude agrees each is ready, **send them yourself** on Upwork and note the Connect cost.
