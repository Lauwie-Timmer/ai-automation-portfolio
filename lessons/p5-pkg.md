## topic: What a package is | what
Many small clients don't want to "hire a developer". They want **a defined result at a known price**. A **package** says:
- what you **deliver**,
- what's **included** and what **isn't**,
- how **long** it takes,
- what it **costs**,
- what you **need from the client**.

Upwork lets you list fixed-price packages that clients can buy directly. The name and rules change (it has been called Project Catalog and project packages), so **check what your account offers**. The same packages work for direct outreach in p5-7.

## topic: Three good first packages | three
Pick narrow and repeatable, built from things you have already made:
1. **Lead-capture workflow:** form to sheet or CRM plus a notification (Phase 2 and Project A).
2. **AI inbox sorter:** classify and route incoming messages, with a review route for uncertain ones (p3-2).
3. **Python data report:** pull from one API, clean it, deliver a CSV or sheet plus a README (Project B).

**Each package must be backed by a project you've built or will build.** If you haven't built it yet, write which project (A, B or C) will prove it. Don't sell what you can't show.

## topic: Pricing, assumptions and exclusions | price
- **Price** with your p5-5 method: estimated hours with a buffer, times your rate, plus the platform fee so your take-home is right.
- Offer **at most a basic and a bigger version.** Don't invent more tiers.
- State **assumptions** (the client has the accounts, sample data arrives within 2 days).
- State **exclusions** (hosting, ongoing maintenance, more than 2 rounds of changes).

Write the three packages in `offers.md`: **name, who it is for, what's included, what isn't, delivery time, price, and what you need from the client.** Check each against your real skills, then ask Claude to review them as a small-business owner. Done when the three packages have honest scope, a price that follows your own method, and a project behind each.
