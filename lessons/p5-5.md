## topic: Hourly versus fixed price | types
- **Hourly** suits unclear work: you bill the time. The client carries the risk of it taking longer.
- **Fixed price** suits small, well-defined work and is usually easier for a client to say yes to. **You carry the risk of a wrong estimate.**

On Upwork, protection differs (check the Help Center for current rules before you accept either):
- **Fixed price:** the client funds a **milestone first** (escrow), you deliver once it's funded.
- **Hourly:** Upwork's payment protection generally depends on **tracking time with Upwork's own time tracker**; hours logged elsewhere may not be covered.

## topic: The estimate formula | estimate
1. **List the tasks.**
2. **Estimate hours** for each.
3. Add about **50% buffer** while you're learning (setup, testing, client delays).
4. Multiply by your **rate**.

Worked example: tasks add up to **8 hours**. With the buffer, 8 × 1.5 = **12 hours**. At 20 euros an hour, 12 × 20 = **240 euros** (the quote before fees).

If you don't add the buffer you will be paid for 8 hours and work 12.

## topic: The Upwork fee | fee
Upwork takes a **service fee** from your earnings. It is **variable** (sources in 2026 say 0% to 15% depending on the contract, locked when you send the proposal; an average of about 10 to 13% is reported). It's **shown before you apply**, so read it each time and add it to your price.

How to price it in: if you want to **take home 240 euros** and the fee is 10%, charge 240 ÷ 0.9 = about **267 euros**. At 15% it is 240 ÷ 0.85 = about **283 euros**. The wrong shortcut (adding 10% on top) leaves you short.

Compare: 8 hours at 20 euros = 160 euros; a 10% fee leaves you 144 euros.

## topic: Scope, exclusions and milestones | scope
**Scope** is the number one cause of bad freelance jobs when it's unclear. Write:
- **What is included** (the deliverables, as a list).
- **What is not included** (exclusions: hosting, ongoing maintenance, new features).
- **How many rounds of changes** (for example 2).
- **What you need from the client**, and by when (access, sample data).
- **Anything outside is a new quote.**

**Milestones:** split bigger jobs into stages with payment per stage, so both sides are protected. **Don't start work on a milestone that isn't funded.**

5-line scope example for Project B: (1) a Python script that fetches EUR/USD and GBP rates for N days; (2) a CSV and a printed summary; (3) 3 tests and a README; (4) excludes hosting, scheduling and charts; (5) 2 rounds of changes, sample accounts needed by day 2.
