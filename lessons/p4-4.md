## topic: The five parts of a case study | parts
A case study is **150 words a client can read in a minute**. It has five parts:

1. **Problem**, in the client's words (what hurt).
2. **What I built** (one or two sentences).
3. **Tools** (n8n, Supabase, Gmail, Python...).
4. **Result**, exact. Say if it was sample data or a prototype.
5. **What I'd do next**.

Example (about 90 words): *"A small business owner kept missing waitlist signups. I built an n8n workflow that checks a Supabase table every 15 minutes, logs each new signup to a Google Sheet and emails the owner once per signup, plus a weekly count. Tools: n8n, Supabase, Google Sheets, Gmail. Result: tested with fake signups, each produced exactly one notification and the weekly count matched. It's a prototype on sample data. Next: connect the real signup table with a read-only key and add retry alerts."*

## topic: Specific beats impressive, and never invent | honest
- **Specific beats impressive.** "Notifies the owner within 15 minutes of each signup and sends a weekly count" is better than "streamlined operations". A client can check the first.
- **Never invent** numbers, testimonials or clients. If you have no result number yet, describe what the tests showed (for example "20 of 20 test emails routed correctly").
- **Label honestly:** "prototype", "sample data", "built for practice". Clients and Upwork trust honest portfolios, and honesty is also what makes your friends' business credible.
- A vague phrase ("robust", "seamless", "cutting-edge") is a sign there is nothing concrete to say. Cut it or replace it with a fact.

**The editing step:** after your first draft, ask for a critique, then **cut 20% of the words** from each. Short and checkable wins.
