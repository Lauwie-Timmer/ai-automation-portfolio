## topic: How clients find you | how-clients-find-you
Clients on Upwork search for a **problem** ("automate lead follow-up", "connect my forms to a spreadsheet"), not for a person. Your profile has to match what they type and what they need.

Two places carry most of the weight:
- the **headline**, one line under your name that appears in search results
- the **overview**, a short text at the top of your profile

A specific headline beats a general one. "Freelancer" says nothing. A good headline says **who you help**, **what you do** and **with which tools**, for example "Automations for small shops: n8n, Python and AI, so forms and orders stop being typed by hand". It is fine to be narrow now. You can widen later when real clients tell you what sells.

Q: Why does a specific headline beat a general one?
A: Clients search for a problem, and a specific headline shows I solve that problem. A general one doesn't stand out or match their search.
Q: What three things does a good headline say?
A: Who I help, what I do, and with which tools.

## topic: Only claim what you can show | honest-claims
Overclaiming gets found out in the first call and costs you the job and your reputation. Claim only what you can point to.

What you can honestly point to now: Python and API work, a mathematics degree, and building a real app with AI-assisted development. Do not claim years of agency experience, or tools you haven't used.

Vague claims are weak too. "Passionate problem solver" can't be checked. "Built an app with an API backend and AI-assisted code" can.

A test for every sentence: *could a client ask me to prove this?* If yes and you can, keep it. If not, remove it.

Q: Which of these is an overclaim for you: "mathematics degree", "5 years running an automation agency", "built an app with AI-assisted development"?
A: "5 years running an automation agency". The other two are things I can show.
Q: What is the test for any sentence in your overview?
A: Could a client ask me to prove it, and could I?

## topic: The three-part overview | overview
The overview has three parts, in about three sentences:

1. **The problem** you solve: what is painful for the client.
2. **How** you solve it: your method and tools.
3. **Proof**: what you've actually built or studied. This gets stronger after Phase 4 when you have portfolio projects.

Write it for someone who doesn't know the tools. "n8n" can appear, but explain what it does for them: "connects your forms, sheets and email so repeated work happens by itself".

Write it in your own words from your real experience. That is why the step starts with Claude interviewing you: your wording should come from what you've really done.

A: The problem I solve, how I solve it, and proof.

A: So the wording comes from my real experience instead of invented claims.

## topic: Doing it | doing-it
Do these yourself. Only you can do the account parts, and Upwork allows one account only, so don't create a second one.

1. Look at your profile in the browser but change nothing yet. Check whether Upwork shows pending steps: identity verification, profile review, a payment method. These can take days, so start them now.
2. Create `C:\dev\automation-portfolio\upwork-profile.md`. Write **3 different headline options** and a **3-sentence overview**.
3. Ask Claude to interview you first (the prompt on the dashboard).
4. Pick the headline you like best.

Done when you have one headline you would put on the profile, and an overview in which Claude can't find vague claims or overclaims.
