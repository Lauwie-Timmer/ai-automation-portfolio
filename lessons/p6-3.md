## topic: GDPR: you are usually a processor | gdpr
If a client's workflow handles **personal data of their customers**, the client is the **controller** (decides why and how data is used) and you are usually a **processor** (handles it for them).

What that means in practice:
- Use **as little personal data as possible.**
- **Keep it out of prompts** when you can.
- **Don't store more than needed.**
- Have the client **agree to how you handle it**, ideally in a **short written agreement.**
- **Don't process health data** without proper advice (it's a special category).

Write a one-page `client-data-rules.md`: what data you accept, what you refuse, and how you handle keys. This isn't legal advice: ask a professional for a specific client.

## topic: The n8n licence | licence
n8n is under the **Sustainable Use License**. The licence text says you may use the software **only for your own internal business purposes or for non-commercial or personal use**, and may distribute it only free of charge for non-commercial purposes.

n8n's **Community license FAQ** gives practical examples (checked October 2026):
- **Allowed:** charging customers for **workflow creation, setup and maintenance services**; building automations for clients who can't create them; running n8n as an internal backend for your own product; running several instances of the community version.
- **Not allowed:** hosting n8n as a service **and letting clients build workflows** in it; letting external users configure workflows through any interface; white-labelling n8n or offering it as is; using enterprise features without a licence.

The key question is **who builds and changes the workflows.** If **you** keep control of the logic and the client only sees results, consulting is allowed. If the client owns and pays for the instance (their Cloud account or server), you building on it is fine. **Read the licence yourself, and ask n8n if your case is unclear.** Write 3 sentences on what it means for how you work.

## topic: AI Act transparency and your credentials | aiact
**EU AI Act, Article 50:** the transparency rules for AI that talks to people apply from **2 August 2026**. For a chatbot: tell people at the **start** that they're talking to an AI unless it is obvious, and offer a human route (p4-3's checklist). The duty falls first on whoever provides the system, so if you build the bot you should act as if it's yours. Rules and dates can change; check the text and ask a professional.

**Own your credentials the right way round:** use the **client's own accounts and API keys** for their automations, never yours, so you never carry their costs or risk.
