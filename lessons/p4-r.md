## topic: Explaining it out loud | explain
You understand something deeply when you can explain it to someone who doesn't know it. **Speaking shows gaps that writing hides.** This redo has no new material: it only tests whether Project A (the signup automation) really sits in your head.

**The task:** dictate a **2-minute explanation** of Project A to a client who runs a small business and has never used n8n. Use voice-to-text (dictation, p0-r), then paste the transcript for grading.

**Rules for the explanation:**
- Don't use the words **item, node, API or webhook** without explaining them in everyday words ("a node is one step in the chain").
- Start with *their* problem (people sign up and you find out too late), then what happens now, then what they get.
- Say what it does **not** do (it's a prototype on fake signups) and what you'd check before using it on real ones.

## topic: What the grader looks for | grading
The Listener compares your transcript with what the project really does and tells you:
1. **What you got wrong** (for example claiming it works on real signups when it only ran on fake ones).
2. **What you skipped** (idempotency, "notified only once per signup", the weekly digest).
3. **Which words a non-technical person wouldn't know.**

Done when the explanation is clear, correct and free of unexplained jargon. If not, fix the gaps and say it again.
