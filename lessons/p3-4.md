## topic: What RAG is and why | rag
**RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)**: first **find** the most relevant pieces of your documents, then **give them to the model** together with the question. The model answers from that text.

Why: an LLM doesn't know your documents, and when it doesn't know, it may hallucinate (p3-1). With RAG the answer comes from **your documents**, not from the model's memory, and you can tell it to say "I don't know" when the text doesn't contain the answer.

```
question → find relevant chunks → [question + chunks] → LLM → answer
```

Q: What does RAG stand for and what does it do?
A: Retrieval-augmented generation: find the relevant pieces of my documents first, then give them to the model with the question.
Q: Which problem does RAG reduce?
A: Hallucination, because the model answers from retrieved text rather than from memory.

## topic: Chunks, embeddings and the vector store | chunks-embeddings
- **Chunking**: a document is split into pieces of a few hundred words. Small enough to be specific, big enough to make sense.
- **Embeddings**: each chunk becomes a **list of numbers** that captures its meaning. Chunks with similar meaning get similar numbers. An embeddings model (for example **Gemini Embeddings**) makes them.
- **Vector store**: a store for these number lists that can quickly find the chunks **closest in meaning** to a question.

Search by meaning, not by exact words: a question "How much does it cost?" can find a chunk saying "Pricing: free during beta".

Quality depends on the source text, the chunk size, and the **instructions** to answer only from the context and say "I don't know" otherwise.

Q: What is an embedding?
A: A list of numbers that represents the meaning of a chunk of text.
Q: What does the vector store do for a question?
A: It finds the chunks whose meaning is closest to the question.

## topic: Simple Vector Store and its limits | simple-vector-store
n8n's **Simple Vector Store** keeps the chunks **in memory**. Its modes include **Insert Documents** and **Retrieve Documents (As Tool for AI Agent)**. Every mode needs a **Memory Key**: a name you choose (we use `faq`). A second workflow can use what the first stored by using the **same Memory Key** (and the same embeddings model).

Limits you must know:
- **All data is lost when n8n restarts.** After every restart you must run the loader again.
- n8n recommends it for **development only**. Real projects use a database such as Supabase.

**Docker and files:** n8n runs in a container, so it can't see files on your Windows disk unless you mount a folder with another `-v` flag. To avoid that for now, you paste the FAQ text into the workflow.

## topic: Finding why an answer is wrong | debugging-rag
When a RAG bot is wrong, the cause is one of three things. Work out which:
- **The chunks**: the answer isn't in any chunk, or the text is split badly (a fact cut in half).
- **The retrieval**: the right chunk exists but wasn't found (check what the tool returned in Logs).
- **The prompt**: the right chunk was found but the model ignored it or made something up (tighten the system prompt).

Method: open the **Logs**, look at what the retrieval tool returned for the question, and compare it with the right answer in your FAQ.

Done when the bot answers the 5 in-document questions correctly and says it doesn't know for the 3 out-of-document ones, and you can explain chunking, embeddings and why the system prompt matters.
know: Tell chunk, retrieval and prompt problems apart
Q: The bot gave a wrong answer. How do you find out whether the cause is the chunks, the retrieval or the prompt?
A: Look in the Logs at what the retrieval returned. If the right text wasn't there it's chunks or retrieval; if it was there but ignored it's the prompt.
